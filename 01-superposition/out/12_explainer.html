Experiments 12 and 13 · person → sport

Where does a network keep a fact, and can we rewrite it?

A small block memorises which sport 400 people play. We look for one fact inside it, change that fact by hand, and then give the block enough room that the fact gets a neuron of its own.

The setup

A fact like "Michael Jordan plays basketball" cannot be worked out from the name. It has to be stored. This is the block that stores it: two weight matrices with a ReLU on the hidden neurons in between.

inputFirst-name vector + last-name vector. 16 random numbers, frozen.
W1 + ReLUHidden neurons. Each one fires for some name pairs. Their values are the vector h.
W2One column per neuron: its vote for each of 8 sports.
scoresThe votes are summed. The highest score wins.

There are 400 facts and 64 neurons, so no fact can have a neuron to itself. His answer only comes out right when the votes of all his neurons are added together.

The edit

To make him a baseball player we add a change to two rows of W2, so that his basketball and baseball scores swap. The only choice is which pattern to write into those rows.

Simple edit
ΔW2 = δ hᵀ / (h·h)
Write his own activations h into the rows.other people change sport, including Magic Johnson.
Careful edit
ΔW2 = δ uᵀ / (u·h), u = C⁻¹h
Write a pattern that points at him and is as perpendicular to everyone else as possible.other people change sport.

Anyone else is moved by δ times their overlap with the written pattern. That overlap is the same interference as in the pentagon. Use the last two buttons on the table above to see both edits.

What we learn

The fact is in the weights, and we can prove it. Changing the weights changes the fact.

With too few neurons, a fact is not in one neuron. It is a sum of votes from neurons that are each shared with a hundred other people.

Sharing is what makes editing risky. The simple edit breaks other facts in proportion to how much their activations overlap with his. Cancelling that overlap fixes it, and more neurons means less overlap to cancel.

Give the network room and a reason to stay quiet, and the facts separate. The same table, stored in a wide sparse layer, puts Michael Jordan on one neuron that fires for nobody else. A sparse autoencoder applies this idea to a model we cannot retrain: a wide, sparse layer trained on top of its activations.

What this does not show

  • Michael Jordan is a clean case for the edit. At 64 neurons the average careful edit changes about 3 other facts.
  • The 600-neuron block is close to a lookup table by size. Its point is the contrast with the 64-neuron block. Width alone, with no pressure to stay quiet, changes nothing.
  • In a real language model, being able to edit a fact at a layer does not prove the fact is stored there. Here the block is the only place it can be.
  • Most of the 400 people are made up, with random sports. The block is memorising, not understanding.
